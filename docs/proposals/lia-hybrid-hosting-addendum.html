2. Why LIA asked for hybrid hosting

The parent proposal (Section 10) already said: the school remains the owner of its student data; another school cannot open LIA records; snapshots do not copy passwords. LIA has now made the hosting rule sharper:

Records that can identify a child, a medical fact, a mark, or a protection case must sit on a server in the Academy — not as the master copy in a public cloud. Everything else — the app people download, the live bus map, notices the school chooses to publish, system updates — may live on the MaJo-Bridge cloud so phones and home internet still work.

That is a normal request for an international school. It is not a second product. It is a placement of the same desks the FINAL proposal already listed.

Plain sentence for the Board. The markbook, the safeguarding file, and the child’s date of birth live in LIA’s server room. The parent still opens the same app. The cloud carries the road to that app; it does not become the school’s filing cabinet.

3. The hybrid picture in one page

Think of two rooms connected by a locked corridor.

LIA school server (on campus)

A computer (or small server pair) that LIA owns, in a locked room LIA controls. This is the vault.

  • Source of truth for student identity, marks, health, safeguarding, discipline, and counseling.
  • Teachers on campus can keep working if the internet to the outside world is slow.
  • LIA IT can see the machine, the power, and the Friday snapshot.

MaJo-Bridge cloud server

The hosted platform the Academy already inspected at https://mayabela.pages.dev. This is the road and the workshop.

  • App updates, login routing by School ID, support tools.
  • Services that need the public internet (live bus GPS, parent phones off campus).
  • Only the data LIA has allowed to leave the vault — never a second master of marks or safeguarding.
One login, two rooms. A teacher, parent, or director still signs in with School ID + phone/username + password. The product then asks one question: “Is this a sensitive LIA record?” If yes, it reads and writes the school server. If no, it uses the cloud. Nobody is asked to keep two systems or two student ids.

4. What stays on the LIA school server

“Sensitive” here means: a record that names a child or a family, a medical or protection fact, a mark, or a staff file LIA treats as internal. These collections have their master copy on the Academy server. The cloud is not allowed to become a second filing cabinet for them.

4.1 Student identity and family (SIS)

  • Legal name, preferred name, student id, date of birth, sex, house, class, campus.
  • Parent and guardian names, phones, emails, and the approved parent-link.
  • Medical flag and the health record the nurse or counselor keeps.
  • Admissions file until the child is enrolled; alumni file after they leave.

4.2 Academic record

  • Weighted markbook: homework, quiz, midterm, exam — the numbers themselves.
  • Exam papers and attempts that carry a score into the markbook.
  • Report cards and the approve → publish trail (who entered, who approved, when the parent may see it).
  • Attendance register (present, late, absent) and the at-risk flag that is computed from marks + absence.
  • Lesson-plan comments that name a class or a teacher evaluation score, if LIA classifies those as internal.

4.3 Care, conduct, and protection

  • Safeguarding / child-protection cases — school server only. Not copied to the cloud. Not visible to parents, students, or classroom teachers.
  • Counseling notes, IEP / special-needs files, college-guidance notes.
  • Discipline and grievance files on the DoSA register.
  • Student documents the school uploaded (passport copy, transfer letter) if LIA marks them sensitive.

4.4 Staff files LIA treats as sensitive

  • HR records and payroll numbers, if the Academy does not want salary or tax files off campus.
  • Role allocations still follow the parent proposal: a teacher does not open safeguarding even on the school server.

4.5 Cameras

CCTV footage stays on the NVR / cameras LIA already owns. The Admin CCTV desk is a window, not a second video archive in the cloud.

If the internet to the outside world fails for an hour. Homeroom can still take attendance and a teacher can still enter marks, because those writes land on the school server inside the campus network. Parents at home will wait until the corridor to the cloud is open again. That is the honest trade of a vault: the school keeps working; the home app waits.

5. What stays on the MaJo-Bridge cloud

The cloud is the part of the system that must meet the public internet — phones, the live site, updates, and support. It holds services and non-sensitive or explicitly published copies, not the school’s master student file.

6. How the two servers work together

6.1 The locked corridor

The school server and the cloud talk only over an encrypted link (HTTPS). The cloud cannot browse the vault like a shared folder. It may ask for a specific published page — “show this parent the report card the Section Director already approved” — and the school server answers yes or no.

6.2 One student id

Creating a child still happens once, on the SIS that lives on the school server. LMS, attendance, markbook, fees, bus list, and support all use that id — the same integration story as Section 9 of the parent proposal. Hybrid does not split the child into “cloud student” and “campus student.”

6.3 Approve-and-publish still holds

A teacher enters a mark on the school server. Leadership approves on the school server. Only then may a limited published copy be visible to the linked parent through the cloud door. Draft marks never sit in the cloud waiting to leak.

6.4 Roles do not get weaker

Hybrid is not a shortcut around Section 8 of the parent proposal. A Grade 8 teacher still does not open safeguarding — whether she is on campus Wi-Fi or at home. A parent still sees only approved children. Another school on the same cloud still cannot open LIA.

6.5 What a morning looks like

  • On campus. Homeroom opens the same attendance desk. The write lands on the LIA server. DoSA oversight reads the same register.
  • Marking. Scores go into the on-campus markbook. The Section Director approves there. The parent app shows the mark only after publish.
  • At home. The parent opens the same Android app or web door. The cloud checks the login, then asks the school server for the published page only.
  • Bus. The driver shares GPS through the cloud map. The passenger list that names children is still an LIA-server list.

7. Security, backups, and who holds the keys

Question the Board will askAnswer in this hybrid
Who owns the student data?Lebawi International Academy. MaJo-Bridge operates the software. The school owns the records. That sentence from the parent proposal does not change.
Where is the master copy?On the LIA school server for every collection listed in Section 4.
Can MaJo staff open a child’s mark from the cloud console?No. Support sees health and login issues. Opening a named student row on campus needs LIA’s named officer and the school-server role — not a cloud browse.
What if the school server disk fails?LIA IT writes the Friday snapshot from Go-live (parent proposal Sections 10 and 14). We add a second copy on a second disk or school PC that LIA controls. Platform-owner restore is a drill LIA schedules — it does not move the master into the public cloud.
What if someone steals a laptop?The vault is the server, not the teacher’s laptop. The laptop holds a session, not the whole SIS. MFA remains available for leadership and finance (parent proposal Section 10).
Encryption?HTTPS on the corridor. Disk encryption on the school server. The hosted cloud encrypts what it is allowed to hold (sessions, published pages, health signals).
Audit?Who changed a mark or opened a safeguarding file is logged on the school server. Leadership and QA open that log as in the parent proposal.

Consent, access requests, and erasure requests stay on the Go-live desk. If a parent asks to erase a file, staff process it on the school server — the place the file actually lives.

10. Company experience that continues from the parent proposal

The FINAL proposal introduced Majo Bridge Technologies and Events as the designer and operator of MaJo Bridge e-School OS. This addendum continues that introduction with the work the same company already delivers — so LIA can see that hybrid hosting and new desks are not a first experiment.

10.1 Fenote Raey Academy

Fenote Raey Academy (ፍኖተ ራእይ አካዳሚ) is a school implementation of the same MaJo e-School Bridge family: administration, teachers, parents, students, and drivers on one School ID. That work is how we practise a full school operating system for a second academy — branding, roles, module mix — without writing a second product. LIA remains its own isolated tenant. Fenote Raey cannot open LIA records; LIA cannot open Fenote Raey records.

10.2 GYMOS — gym software

GYMOS is membership and gym-floor software: who belongs, who visited, who is allowed on the floor. It is a different industry from a school, and that is the point. The habit is the same as LIA’s SIS: one record, roles, a live desk instead of a spreadsheet. When a gym asks for “member files on our machine, the app in the cloud,” that is the same hybrid sentence LIA is asking now.

10.3 Post Printing Company — ERP

For Post Printing Company we deliver an ERP-style operational system: orders, production, and the screens a print house actually uses. Printing is not education, but it is a business that also keeps customer jobs and files it does not want as a public-cloud master. That delivery is evidence we place ledgers on the machine the client controls, and keep the shared tools where they belong.

10.4 Tena — health and fitness app

Tena is a health and wellbeing / fitness app for people who train and track how they feel. It is a consumer product beside the school ERP. It shows the company can ship a phone app that people use every day — the same kind of door LIA parents will open — while keeping health-type facts under a clear rule.

10.5 Websites and other ongoing projects

MaJo-Bridge designs, builds, and maintains client websites — public pages and the back-office those sites need. Further builds are in delivery now. LIA is not the last item on a finished list. The company is actively shipping, which is why a hybrid cutover and a later new desk can be scheduled instead of promised as a future rewrite.

What this means for LIA. The FINAL proposal showed the academic system is already built. This addendum shows the company that built it also runs another school (Fenote Raey Academy), a gym product (GYMOS), a printing ERP (Post Printing Company), a health and fitness app (Tena), websites, and ongoing work. Hybrid hosting is therefore a configuration we stand behind with a team that already splits “what the client must hold” from “what the cloud may carry.”
