1. Executive summary

Overall conclusion: MaJo Bridge is not yet conformant with CMCSRS v2.0 as an operating organisation. This standard is a mandatory requirement catalogue, not a 0–100 maturity scorecard. It has no Table 1. Certification is pass/fail on the MUST clauses that apply. Several MUST clauses fail: locally licensed cloud (§6.29.k), organisation-held encryption keys for classified cloud data (§6.29.f), system accreditation before processing classified information (§6.22.a), annual compliance reporting to INSA (§6.24), and designated security-assessment positions under the national clearance scheme (§6.37). Those are not defects in Flutter tests. They are national-process and hosting requirements.

The earlier 86/100 Mature result still stands for the Guideline v1.0 evaluation of the submitted product CSMS. It answers: “does the candidate have a CSMS and closed Major technical NCs?” This report answers: “does the operator meet CMCSRS v2.0 MUST clauses?” Different question, different result.

What still holds from the product work: school isolation and driver need-to-know are CAAT-proven in source; privileged MFA (Admin + Finance) without locking teachers; Maya Assistant DPIA; CSMS pack adopted. Those close many Should technical items. They do not override a MUST on locally licensed cloud.

1.1 What this fail does and does not mean

  • Does: INSA, if they audit MaJo Bridge against CMCSRS v2.0 as a key private operator of a live school system, can refuse organisational certification until the MUST cloud/accreditation/reporting gaps are treated (local CSP or INSA exception; accreditation; annual letter).
  • Does not: cancel Guideline R3 (86/100) on the product candidate; require taking schools offline today; require forcing MFA on classroom teachers; require hiding login logos.

5. Annex B classification used in this audit

CMCSRS v2.0 Annex B has four labels only: TOP SECRET, SECRET, CONFIDENTIAL, PUBLIC. This audit classifies:

  • CONFIDENTIAL: student identity, marks, attendance, health/safeguarding collections, staff HR/payroll in the ERP, service-role key, CSMS pack.
  • PUBLIC: school logos on the login branding bucket (no JWT on the login page); anon/publishable key (not a secret; RLS is the boundary); synthetic CAAT data.
  • SECRET / TOP SECRET: not claimed. Unauthorised disclosure of a school register would damage children and the operator; it is not “exceptionally grave damage to national security” on the Annex B examples (widespread loss of life, military capability). If INSA later directs a higher label, MNC-V2-04 becomes stricter (TOP SECRET accreditation authority is INSA — §6.22.l).

7. Corrective action to reach CMCSRS v2.0 organisational conformance

Guideline-style “close Minors in git this afternoon” will not flip this result. The blocking work is national and hosting:

  1. Treat MNC-V2-01/02/03: ask INSA in writing whether the current host is an approved exception; if not, plan a locally licensed CSP or dedicated in-country instance. Keep the on-prem path for schools whose TOR already forbids shared foreign tenancy.
  2. Treat MNC-V2-04: start accreditation: system owner letter, residual-risk statement, this report, Guideline R3, CSMS pack, CAAT evidence. Do not wait for a mid-day production cutover.
  3. Treat MNC-V2-05: annual compliance declaration from the organisation head to the equivalent authority, copy to INSA, listing the residual MUSTs above.
  4. Treat MNC-V2-06: fill DSAP files under the National Clearance Scheme when INSA issues the channel.
  5. Then the notified staging→production SQL window (OBS-V2-PR) so the operating environment matches the hardened source, followed by the independent pentest letter already tracked in CSMS-TEV-001.
Cost-effective principle (Part I §4.4.c). Do not force teacher MFA, hide logos, or invent a blockchain/IoT CSMS to “look complete.” Those clauses are N/A or already treated. Spend the next effort on local cloud / INSA exception and accreditation — those are the MUST failures.

8. Statement of the auditor

This evaluation uses the auditee-supplied Critical Mass Cyber Security Requirement Standard version 2.0 as the criteria, acting in the form of a regulatory-style auditor for advice before national engagement. It is not a gazetted INSA certificate and it does not authorise operation as if certified under CMCSRS v2.0.

Findings are evidence-based on the candidate repository, the adopted CSMS (including CSMS-CMCSRS-001), and the prior Guideline R3 fieldwork of 9 November 2026 (Africa/Nairobi). Where a MUST could not be evidenced, it is failed (Part I §1.1 Must = absolute). Live production was excluded from mutation by auditee instruction.

Audit conclusion: MaJo e-School Bridge / MayaBela, as an operating organisation processing CONFIDENTIAL children’s data on a foreign-hosted shared cloud without INSA accreditation or an annual compliance letter, does not yet conform to CMCSRS v2.0. The product CSMS evaluated under Guideline v1.0 remains Mature 86/100 for that other document. Classroom teachers, parents, and drivers were not locked by this work.
